A lobster tank top and a fringed net skirt entered the same week. That's just what happened.

## The Lobster Tank

Diana Unraveled's The Lobster Tank is exactly what you think it is: a boxy colorwork tank with a full lobster on the front. Not a small lobster. Not a subtle lobster reference. A lobster. The construction is clean and the silhouette is simple — the lobster is the entire decision and it is the correct one. I've seen it worked in red-on-black, red-on-cream, and a yellow-on-black situation that is deeply unwell in the best possible way. Every single version is committed. This is the good kind of knitting project.

## Kalavale Skirt

Maija Pihlajaoja makes a mesh skirt with long looped fringe cascading down the whole length. The construction is a knitted open net, which means it's simultaneously airy and substantial, and the fringe makes it look like something you'd find in a vintage shop and keep forever. The red version is definitive. I will not be taking questions.

## Homecoming Quilt Vest

Katryn Seeburger's Homecoming Quilt Vest is stranded colorwork, but the motif is a quilt-block grid — stars, pinwheels, and geometric patches in dozens of colors. Every version I've seen uses a different palette and every version looks like an actual patchwork quilt someone decided to wear. It's maximalist but it's not chaotic. The structure keeps it. 32–68 inches.

## The Pixie situation

Knitty Bae designed the Pixie Sweater twice: once in knit, once in crochet. Same wide-stripe oversized silhouette, different craft. Both are made to measure, which means you size it to yourself rather than picking from a size range. Ravelry has the knit version and the crochet version listed separately — they're both there, both worth seeing.

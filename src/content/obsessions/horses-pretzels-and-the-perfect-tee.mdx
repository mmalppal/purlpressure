Three tees this week. I didn't plan it — it just happened. And all three are completely different answers to the same question, which I think proves the tee is worth asking about every single season.

The Silvester Tee by Anna Hermann Sørensen is the clean one. Mock neck, short sleeve, stockinette. Three colorways: navy, heather purple, cream. No decoration. No explanation. It just fits right, and that's harder than it looks.

The Shell Tee from Wool & Beyond goes a different direction — split neckline, gathered yoke, cream linen-weight yarn. Reads like a blouse. The kind of thing people ask about when you're wearing it because they don't immediately clock that it's handknit.

The Uptight Turtle Tee from HOC is a cropped striped turtleneck in magenta and maroon. Tight stripe, tight fit, cropped. A completely different answer to the same question. All three are correct.

## The horse sweater

Neigh by Caitlin Hunter has running horses across the yoke in three colorways — cream and rust, cream and blue, maroon and cream. Someone photographed it while standing next to an actual horse. The sweater and the horse matched in color. I don't know how to follow that up so I won't try.

## The pretzel cable

The Pretzel Sweater from Hello Knitters has a cable panel shaped like a pretzel. When I type that out loud it sounds like a novelty item. It is not a novelty item. It's a clean, wearable sweater with a cable that happens to be shaped like something you eat with mustard. It works because the rest of the sweater lets it work.

## The last two

Sofi's Bollum Sweater by LittleSofiKnits: brown, fitted, high neck, fuzzy yarn. It photographs like a mood. You know the whole vibe of the person wearing it just from the thumbnail.

The Juuli Sweater from Knitters & Friends is stripes in purple, grey, and mauve, slightly oversized. A color combination that has no business working as well as it does and absolutely works.

Two Mei Bruns patterns in the same week. I'm not sorry. She's doing something right now and I want to be paying attention.

This week is broadly about: color, ruffles, hardware I didn't expect, and a very large daisy.

## The Mei Bruns situation

The Fruits Cardigan has strawberries and apples in the yoke. The Lettuce Sweater has ruffled lettuce-leaf hems at the collar, cuffs, and body hem. Both are sized 28–60-something inches. Both are fully committed to their concept without being precious about it.

They are not the same vibe. The Fruits Cardigan is cozy and classic — the kind of thing you'd wear to a farmers market and feel extremely correct about. The Lettuce Sweater is doing something different. The ruffle isn't decorative; it *is* the sweater. It looks like the sweater grew at the edges. I think about this more than I should.

## Tago

amimonology makes a sweater called Tago that has grommets. Metal eyelet grommets, the hardware kind, set directly into the raglan seam line from shoulder to cuff. Every version I've seen uses a different color — jewel tones, earth tones, a whole rainbow gradient along one sleeve. The base sweater is dead simple. The grommets are the entire statement.

This is the kind of pattern that's been on Ravelry since 2017, accruing quietly enthusiastic project pages, waiting for the moment everyone discovers it. This is that moment.

## The big daisy

Lisa Richardson's Exotic from Rowan Magazine 75. The colorwork motif is a large, graphic daisy in cream on a sage-green ground. The scale of the flower relative to the sweater is exactly right — not so small it reads as ditsy, not so big it's a statement piece. It just looks like someone made a sweater with a flower on it and had a good time doing it.

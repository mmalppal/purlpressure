Some weeks I save patterns because they're beautiful. Some weeks I save patterns because they have *something to say* and they're not being polite about it.

This is the second kind of week.

## YUCK

The 4-Letter Sweater by Lindsay Degen. Pick a word: YUCK. HOPE. YEAH. OKAY. The pattern gives you the structure; you choose the message. The model in the original photos is wearing YUCK on a yellow sweater, polka-dot pants, and a tiny beaded hat. She looks like she's having the best day of anyone's life.

I want to make YUCK in a dark mustard and wear it to the grocery store.

## The anti-surveillance one

Ottilia Westerlund's Anti-Surveillance Jumper is a real pattern you can knit in real yarn and wear in the real world, and it will theoretically confuse facial recognition cameras. The black-and-white pixel-art pattern creates visual noise that breaks up the face-detection bounding box. I found this out from the pattern notes.

It's also free.

## Kaffe

Foolish Virgins is a Kaffe Fassett pattern from approximately the year I was born. It's also free on Ravelry. The colorwork is fully unhinged — dense intarsia figures, multiple saturated colors per row, zero restraint. It is the exact opposite of the soft neutral aesthetic that dominates Ravelry right now, and I think that's the point.

## The hands

Chantal Belisle's *Hands* has two intarsia hands reaching up from the shoulders of the cardigan. They're reaching toward something. I don't know what. The pattern doesn't say. I respect that.

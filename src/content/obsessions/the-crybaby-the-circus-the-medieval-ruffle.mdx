The Crybaby Sweater came first. I don't know what I expected from something with that name, but the actual garment is surprisingly composed — the kind of thing where you look at the sleeve placement and realize someone made a real decision there. Bea Fischer-Harrison. I've been thinking about it since I found it.

Then I fell into a color spiral. The COLORFIELD VEST by my.maane is color blocking that reads like an abstract painting. Simple from a distance, considered up close. The name is in all caps in the original and the vest earns that.

## About the ruffle

The Medieval Ruffle Blouse from Fabel Knitwear shouldn't work as a name and it completely does. Medieval. Ruffle. Blouse. Three words I would not have predicted together in a pattern title, in that order, describing something I would actually wear. The silhouette makes sense in a way I can't explain without just telling you to look at it.

## The rest of June

The Circus Blouse by Sofia Holck Petersen has volume in the sleeves I don't fully understand yet but want to figure out with yarn. The Resize Soft Sweater from Traditionel Strik DK has a name that explains almost nothing and a construction that explains everything once you look closely.

Yardana by Teti Lutsak is quieter — a stitch pattern doing something slightly unusual in a way that rewards a second look. The Cool Down T-Shirt Dress from Handmade by Hanna Louise is exactly what it sounds like: knit this for the days when you need to not think about structure.

And the Teddy Stripes Sweater by Klara Nilsson is stripes in the blue range, the shape of something you'd find at the back of a very good closet. She keeps making things I want to own. This is another one.

June has opinions. I'm taking notes.

Some weeks the theme picks itself. This week it's patchwork — twice, unapologetically — plus a bobble sweater that photographs like an engineering diagram and a colorwork yoke that showed up with a bunny in the reference photos.

## The patchwork problem (a good problem)

Both the Key Cardigan Tee and the Patchwork Cardigan are doing the piecework thing this week, and they're doing it completely differently. Lindsay Degen's key-pattern panel reads like a logo — bold, graphic, short-sleeved, no apologies. Laura Dalgaard's cardigan is the quieter cousin: stripes and solid squares pieced together like an actual quilt, every block a small decision. I want both. I will knit neither before winter. This is fine.

Tine Rousing's Blouse 3 is technically a third patchwork entry if you squint — kaleidoscope colorwork buttoned all the way down the front — and it's the one I keep opening the tab for.

## The bobbles

Alice Hoyle's Clover Sweater is deceptively simple: all-over bobble clovers on a relaxed V-neck, knit in a soft cream that makes the texture do all the talking. The back view in the project photos is somehow more compelling than the front. I don't fully understand why. I've stopped trying to.

## The cables, the turtleneck

Skonsa's Space Between Sweater is a meditative-knit, statement-piece turtleneck with intricate cabling that pulls the fabric in just enough to look engineered rather than accidental. German short rows required. Worth it.

## The bunny sweater

saskie&co's clairdelune is a circular-yoke colorwork jumper in ten adult sizes, unisex, sized by actual final chest measurement instead of vague letters — my favorite kind of pattern page. The designer's own project photos feature his rabbit wearing a matching bunny hug. I have no further commentary. I simply needed you to know this.

The poncho was first. I came across it and just sat with it for a while. Not because ponchos are new — they're not — but because this one gets the proportions right in a way that's harder than it looks. Mohair, simple drape, no fuss. The Mohair Poncho by amyisknitting has one job and it does it well.

Then the cables. The Wavvy Cable Sweater by Lindsay Degen has panels that move across the body in actual waves. Not the kind of cable that reads as texture from across the room — the kind you have to look at for a second before you understand what you're seeing. Good design does that.

## The 3D glasses

I have to talk about the Knitted 3D Glasses. Christine R. Hansen designed knitted versions of the red-and-blue cinema glasses and they look exactly right. Extremely specific, completely realized, deeply committed to the bit. I don't know who this is for but I'm glad it exists.

## French Golf

Beth Skwarecki named this one well. Short-sleeve striped cardigan with the energy of a person who plays a sport you've heard of but don't fully understand, on a coast somewhere, in the 1960s. It's sporty without being athletic. Refined without trying. The French Golf.

## The rest of it

The Neighbors Sweater from Postcard Knits has a colorwork yoke that actually looks like a neighborhood from above — small geometric motifs, warm palette, satisfying repetition. The Langeland Sweater by Kit Couture is quieter: textured, boxy, natural tones. Named for a Danish island. Makes sense.

The QR-Key by Lisa Bogart is a Fair Isle panel with a functional QR code knitted into it. It scans. I tested the photos. I don't know what I expected but "it works" wasn't my first guess, and yet.

Last: the Amatheia Mockneck Midi Dress by June Lagoon. A full knitted midi dress, mock neck, clean line. The kind of project that makes you reconsider what the medium is capable of.

May is doing something. I'm paying attention.

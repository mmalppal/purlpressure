The ravioli bag came first. Lili Fae designed a crossbody bag with crimped edges and two buttons that is shaped exactly like a piece of pasta. It's knit in chunky yellow and photographed next to a wheel of brie to confirm the interior dimensions. The brie fits. The pattern is free. I don't know what else to say about this except that it exists and it works.

## Three from Alice Hoyle

This week I ended up with three Alice Hoyle patterns and I'm not going to apologize for it.

The Kaleido Cardigan is colorwork done at full volume — blocks and stripes in ten or twelve shades arranged so that no two versions look alike. The Ziggy Sweater is the opposite: cream and brown, two colors, a textured stitch that reads as woven fabric from across the room. The collar and cuffs are chocolate. Everything about it is considered.

The Drift Jacket is cables. A big shawl collar you can pull over your chin, diagonal cables moving across the body, and enough weight to feel like a real jacket. All three are from ROWS Knitwear and all three are good for completely different reasons.

## The Twisty Top

Tatsiana's Twisty Top is a sleeveless tank in two-color slip stitch that has the texture of something woven rather than knitted. There's a back button closure. The stitch is the whole point and the stitch is excellent.

## Svetlana Gibadullina went to the garden and the barn

The Heirloom Tomato Pullover is a bright green pullover with red tomatoes across the whole body. Realistic tomatoes. Detailed tomatoes. The kind of tomatoes that make you think about your garden in March. This is a garment that knows what it is.

Then Cluck Couture: a black cardigan covered in white chickens in rows. The chickens have red combs. There are a lot of chickens. Svetlana Gibadullina designed both of these within what appears to be the same general creative direction — flora, fauna, conviction — and it works both times.

A committed week.
